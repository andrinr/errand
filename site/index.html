Scoring notes

Score = 1000 × (levels completed ÷ 5) ÷ (1 + seconds ÷ 120). Completion is weighted by elapsed time; zero completions earn zero points. Times are minutes:seconds. ¹ Average per attempted errand, including failures. Total = compute + accidental subscription charges + consumed goods, purchases, and perks. Subscription charges count in full when triggered; consumed expenses include paper, ink, power, coffee, luxury goods, travel, retreats, and consultants. Salaries and hardware excluded. Click a participant to read their incident report.

Neuron estimates: every human uses ≈86B biological neurons, a rounded whole-brain reference from Azevedo et al. (2009), not an individual measurement. Model values are artificial feed-forward units estimated as assumed parameters × 0.8 ÷ (3 × 8192): 80% feed-forward weights, three matrices per gated unit, and width 8192. These are illustrative architecture assumptions, not verified model sizes or a conversion to biological neurons; attention units and repeated token activations are excluded. Assumed total parameters range from 128B to 3.2T for the frontier entries; hover a count for its input. Effort does not change architecture. Le Chaton-fat is set to 1,000× the largest frontier estimate on this board. Counts do not affect scores.

Data & methodology

A point is Pareto-efficient if no other participant costs the same or less and scores the same or more, with at least one strict improvement. The cost axis is linear from $0–1, logarithmic from $1–100, then exponential to the largest tick. Pareto membership uses full costs and the combined score, not screen distances.

Did the thing actually get done?

Five interactive environments test your ability to do one ordinary thing while the interface actively resists you. Cursed office mode adds tighter timers, interruptions, and interface traps. Practice gives you more time to discover the solution. Two easier bonus games offer shorter challenges. Finish the Update is an unscored, deliberately unwinnable sandbox. The seven other games have completion paths.

01. Give it an errand.

One clear task. A messy environment. No bonus points for explaining why it’s hard.

02. Look for the outcome.

The page came out. The membership ended. The slide appeared. “Done!” is not evidence.

03. Count the collateral.

Record time, cost, and what was left behind.

Satirical benchmark: leaderboard results, effort presets, and costs are invented; games are playable browser simulations. Unaffiliated with the companies shown or ARC-AGI-3.
